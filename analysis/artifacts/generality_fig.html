How many vectors does the shared axis need?

Fit the shared axis on k randomly chosen traits, always excluding the one being scored, then rank its residual. 27 held-out traits × 3 random fit-sets each = 81 trials per k, layer 28. Each curve is one rank bar: the share of trials whose residual put the trait’s own token in the top 1, the top 5, or the top 10 of the whole vocabulary.

Which layer does the residual become readable at?

Median rank of the trait’s own token for the 15 held-out non-animal traits, at every layer. Rank 1 means the trait’s name is the model’s top prediction; ~152,000 is the bottom of the vocabulary.

Not animal-specific. A shared axis fitted on three animals leaves residuals that name trees, four instruments, six abstract ideas, a musical form and a colour — 13 of these 15 held-out traits land in the top 3.

Naming, since it inverts easily: the shared axis is what gets discarded; the residual is what gets read. The shared axis holds ~98% of each vector's variance and decodes to nothing; the residual is the remaining ~12% of its length and carries the identity.

k = 3 is already enough. One vector gets 74/81 into the top 10; three gets 79/81; everything from 5 to 36 sits between 78 and 80. There is no meaningful gain past a handful, which is what you would expect if the shared component is a single direction rather than a subspace that accumulates.

The two misses are tokenizer artifacts, not failures. “maple” begins with the token “map”, and “algorithm” is scored on a first token shared with unrelated words. Both decode to obviously correct content (“Algorithm”, “算法”) just below rank 10.
