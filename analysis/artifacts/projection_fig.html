2. The Venn version

The same decomposition as overlapping sets — the framing to interrogate before using it.

Is the Venn framing good or bad? Useful for one thing, actively misleading for two.

What it gets right: proportion. Trait vectors really are ~97% overlapping, and a reader immediately sees that almost everything these two directions encode is common to both.

Where it misleads. First, area reads as importance, and here the relationship is inverted: the 98.4% region decodes to garbage while the 1.5% sliver puts the trait's own name at rank 1 of 152,000. A Venn invites the conclusion that the slivers are a rounding error, which is the opposite of the finding. Second, sets don't subtract like vectors. Set membership is binary and unordered; these are vectors with an inner product, and the whole result depends on projecting along a direction and keeping what is perpendicular. There is no set operation that corresponds to “orthogonal complement”, and the Venn silently drops the geometry that does the work.

Use it as the opening intuition, never as the explanation. If a Venn appears in a write-up, the vector figure has to appear beside it, and the caption has to say that the small region is the informative one.
